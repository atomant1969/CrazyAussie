Beginner YouTube Curriculum

Parent: Video Roadmap

Related: Video Structure | Upload Checklist

Purpose

This curriculum is the lead-generation engine for the channel. It teaches a complete beginner how to go from no setup to a published YouTube video.

The teaching path is intentionally simple:

  1. Android phone basics.
  2. Recording videos.
  3. Getting videos from phone to PC.
  4. Organizing footage.
  5. Editing videos.
  6. Exporting videos.
  7. Uploading to YouTube.
  8. Choosing thumbnail, title, description, visibility, and playlist.
  9. Publishing and checking the video.
  10. Reading basic results.

The viewer should feel: "I can actually do this."

Teaching Style

The audience is practical beginners. Assume they are not stupid, but they are new.

Teach with:

  • Plain language.
  • One job per video.
  • Visible examples.
  • Repeatable steps.
  • Common mistakes.
  • A clear next video.

Avoid:

  • Creator jargon without explanation.
  • Long theory before showing the task.
  • Fancy gear as the answer to every problem.
  • Overcomplicating the first upload.
  • Making beginners feel behind.

Area 1: Starting YouTube With An Android Phone

Core Message

A beginner does not need a cinema camera to start YouTube. A decent Android phone, clean audio, good light, stable framing, and a simple workflow are enough to make the first videos.

What To Explain

  • What a beginner actually needs to start.
  • Why the phone is good enough for learning.
  • Why audio and lighting matter more than expensive camera specs.
  • Why storage and file transfer matter.
  • Why short test videos are better than planning forever.

Minimum Specs To Teach

Baseline:

  • 1080p recording.
  • 30 fps minimum.
  • 64 GB storage minimum.
  • Rear camera usable in normal daylight.
  • USB-C transfer preferred.
  • Enough battery health to record several takes.

Better:

  • 1080p 60 fps.
  • 4K optional, not required.
  • 128 GB or more storage.
  • External microphone support.
  • Optical or electronic stabilization.
  • Manual exposure/focus lock in the camera app.

Beginner Mistakes

  • Thinking they need a new phone before making the first video.
  • Recording with a dirty lens.
  • Recording with almost no storage space.
  • Recording long takes before testing audio.
  • Using 4K when their computer struggles to edit it.
  • Forgetting battery drain during recording.

Demo Ideas

  • Show a clean-lens vs dirty-lens recording.
  • Show 720p, 1080p, and 4K file size differences.
  • Show a 30-second test recording checklist.
  • Show phone storage before and after recording.

Area 2: Setting Up The Phone Before Recording

Core Message

Most beginner phone footage looks bad because the setup is rushed, not because the phone is bad.

What To Explain

  • Clean the lens.
  • Charge the battery.
  • Free storage space.
  • Turn on Do Not Disturb.
  • Choose landscape or vertical based on video type.
  • Set resolution and frame rate.
  • Lock focus and exposure if the app allows it.
  • Check the background.
  • Check sound before recording the full video.

Recommended Settings

For long-form YouTube:

  • Landscape.
  • 1080p.
  • 30 fps for normal talking videos.
  • 60 fps only if there is movement or action.

For Shorts:

  • Vertical.
  • 1080 x 1920 if available.
  • 30 fps or 60 fps.

For beginners:

  • Start with 1080p.
  • Avoid 4K until storage, transfer, and editing workflow are stable.

Beginner Mistakes

  • Recording vertical footage for a normal long-form video by accident.
  • Recording landscape footage for Shorts without planning the crop.
  • Leaving notifications on.
  • Standing in front of a bright window.
  • Trusting the first take without checking it.
  • Holding the phone by hand for long talking sections.

Demo Ideas

  • Show the same shot with bad background and better background.
  • Show what happens when exposure changes mid-shot.
  • Show vertical vs landscape framing.
  • Show a cheap tripod or improvised stable setup.

Good Video Titles

  • Stop Recording Bad Phone Footage: Do This First

Area 3: Recording Your First Video

Core Message

The first video should be simple. The goal is not perfection; the goal is a complete usable recording.

What To Explain

  • Start with a short video topic.
  • Record a 10-second audio test.
  • Frame yourself with space above the head.
  • Keep the phone stable.
  • Look at the lens, not the screen.
  • Record in short sections if nervous.
  • Pause and restart instead of forcing one perfect take.
  • Capture one extra visual shot for the thumbnail.

Basic First Video Formula

  1. Say what the video is about.
  2. Explain three points.
  3. Recap.
  4. Tell the viewer the next step.

Beginner Mistakes

  • Trying to record a 30-minute first video.
  • Reading stiffly from a script.
  • Looking at themselves on screen instead of the lens.
  • Recording in a noisy room.
  • Forgetting to capture a thumbnail frame.
  • Not checking the first clip before recording everything.

Demo Ideas

  • Record one bad take and one improved take.
  • Show how to restart a sentence cleanly for easier editing.
  • Show how clapping once can help find sync or cut points.
  • Show how to capture a thumbnail pose/frame at the end.

Good Video Titles

  • Your First Talking Video: Simple Phone Recording Setup
  • Record Your First YouTube Video Without Overthinking It

Area 4: Getting Videos From Android To PC

Core Message

A YouTube workflow breaks if footage is hard to find, hard to transfer, or scattered everywhere.

Transfer Methods To Explain

USB cable:

  • Best beginner method.
  • Fast and reliable.
  • Requires unlock and file transfer mode.

Cloud transfer:

  • Useful for small files.
  • Can be slow for large videos.
  • Depends on internet speed and storage limits.

External storage:

  • Useful later.
  • Needs compatible phone and drive.
  • More advanced than USB cable.

Messaging apps:

  • Usually not recommended for source footage.
  • Can compress video and reduce quality.

Folder Structure

Use simple folders:

YouTube/
  001-first-phone-video/
    01-raw/
    02-project/
    03-export/
    04-thumbnail/
    05-shorts/

Beginner Mistakes

  • Editing directly from the phone without backing up.
  • Copying files to random desktop locations.
  • Using messaging apps that compress video.
  • Deleting phone footage before checking the PC copy.
  • Not naming folders by project.

Demo Ideas

  • Show USB transfer step by step.
  • Show where Android camera files usually live.
  • Show a messy folder vs clean project folder.
  • Show how to confirm the copied file plays on PC.

Good Video Titles

  • How to Transfer Videos From Android to PC for YouTube
  • Do Not Start Editing Until You Organize Your Phone Footage
  • The Simple Folder System I Use for YouTube Videos

Area 5: Organizing And Checking Footage

Core Message

Organization saves beginners from losing footage, editing the wrong file, or exporting a broken video.

What To Explain

  • Raw footage stays untouched.
  • Project files go in a separate folder.
  • Exports go in an export folder.
  • Thumbnails have their own folder.
  • Shorts cutdowns have their own folder.
  • Watch the first and last few seconds of each clip.
  • Check audio before editing.
  • Rename clips only if it helps.

Beginner Mistakes

  • Moving files after starting the edit, causing missing media.
  • Deleting raw footage too early.
  • Not knowing which file is the final export.
  • Having several files called final, final2, realfinal.
  • Ignoring audio problems until late.

Demo Ideas

  • Create a new project folder live.
  • Copy clips into the raw folder.
  • Play back a clip and check sound.
  • Show a naming convention.

Good Video Titles

  • How to Organize YouTube Footage Before Editing

Area 6: Choosing Editing Software

Core Message

The best beginner editor is the one that helps the viewer finish and upload videos consistently.

What To Compare

  • Cost.
  • Watermark or no watermark.
  • Computer requirements.
  • Learning curve.
  • Cutting tools.
  • Audio tools.
  • Text/caption tools.
  • Export options.
  • Shorts support.
  • Long-form support.

Software Categories

Easy beginner editors:

  • Good for simple videos.
  • Faster to learn.
  • May be limiting later.

Professional editors:

  • More powerful.
  • Better for long-term growth.
  • Can overwhelm beginners.

Browser editors:

  • Convenient.
  • May depend on internet and account limits.
  • Good for simple projects.

Beginner Mistakes

  • Choosing the most complicated tool first.
  • Switching software every week.
  • Watching tutorials instead of finishing videos.
  • Using software that adds a watermark without realizing.
  • Editing 4K on a weak computer.

Demo Ideas

  • Import the same clip into several editors.
  • Time how long it takes to make the first cut.
  • Export the same clip from each editor.
  • Compare beginner friendliness honestly.

Good Video Titles

Area 7: First Edit

Core Message

The first edit only needs to remove mistakes, keep the message clear, and export properly.

What To Teach

  • Create a project.
  • Import footage.
  • Put clip on timeline.
  • Cut the beginning and end.
  • Remove mistakes.
  • Keep the best take.
  • Add simple text only if needed.
  • Balance audio.
  • Watch the full edit before export.

Beginner Mistakes

  • Adding effects before the basic cut is clean.
  • Leaving long pauses.
  • Cutting too much and making speech unnatural.
  • Making background music louder than voice.
  • Not watching the final edit all the way through.

Demo Ideas

  • Show an unedited clip.
  • Make three basic cuts.
  • Remove one mistake.
  • Add one simple title.

Area 8: Exporting Videos

Core Message

Export settings do not need to be mysterious. Beginners need a clean file that looks good, plays correctly, and uploads without drama.

What To Explain

  • Export means creating the finished video file.
  • Match export resolution to the footage.
  • Use 1080p for beginner long-form videos.
  • Use vertical format for Shorts.
  • Name exports clearly.
  • Watch the exported file before uploading.

Beginner Export Defaults

Long-form:

  • MP4.
  • 1080p.
  • 30 fps if recorded at 30 fps.
  • H.264 if available.

Shorts:

  • MP4.
  • 1080 x 1920 vertical.
  • Under 60 seconds unless intentionally using the current Shorts limit.

Beginner Mistakes

  • Exporting the wrong timeline.
  • Exporting without audio.
  • Exporting at a tiny resolution.
  • Uploading the project file instead of the exported video.
  • Not checking the final file before upload.

Demo Ideas

  • Show where the export button is.
  • Show good beginner settings.
  • Show the final export folder.
  • Play the exported file before upload.

Good Video Titles

Area 9: Uploading To YouTube

Core Message

Uploading is not just selecting a file. The upload page is where the video becomes a YouTube video: title, description, thumbnail, playlist, audience, visibility, and checks.

What To Teach

  • Sign in to YouTube Studio.
  • Click create/upload.
  • Select the exported video file.
  • Wait for upload and processing.
  • Add title.
  • Add description.
  • Choose or upload thumbnail.
  • Add playlist if relevant.
  • Choose audience setting.
  • Set visibility.
  • Publish, schedule, or save.
  • Check the video after upload.

Important Platform Notes

According to YouTube Help, creators can upload from YouTube Studio on desktop, set visibility during upload, and use privacy settings such as public, private, and unlisted. YouTube also notes that verified accounts can access features such as custom thumbnails and videos longer than 15 minutes.

Beginner Mistakes

  • Uploading the wrong file.
  • Publishing before checking title and thumbnail.
  • Leaving the video private by accident.
  • Choosing public before processing is ready.
  • Forgetting to check the video page after upload.
  • Forgetting whether the video is made for kids.

Demo Ideas

  • Upload a test video as private.
  • Show each upload field.
  • Show how the video appears before publishing.
  • Show how to change visibility.

Good Video Titles

Area 10: Thumbnails

Core Message

The thumbnail is the first visual promise of the video. It must be clear, honest, and readable when small.

What To Explain

  • What a thumbnail is.
  • Where it appears.
  • Auto-generated thumbnails vs custom thumbnails.
  • Why custom thumbnails usually give more control.
  • Why YouTube account verification may be needed for custom thumbnails.
  • How to choose a strong frame if using an auto thumbnail.
  • How to make a simple custom thumbnail.
  • How to check it on mobile.

Thumbnail Types

Auto-generated:

  • Fast.
  • Uses frames from the video.
  • Good for first uploads if the frame is clear.
  • Limited control.

Custom:

  • More control.
  • Can be designed before upload.
  • Better for repeatable branding.
  • Requires more effort and may require account verification.

Beginner Thumbnail Rules

  • One clear subject.
  • Clear face, object, or result.
  • High contrast.
  • Minimal text.
  • Readable when small.
  • Must honestly represent the video.
  • Title and thumbnail should not repeat the exact same words unless there is a reason.

Beginner Mistakes

  • Tiny text.
  • Too many objects.
  • Screenshot with no clear subject.
  • Misleading image.
  • Thumbnail that looks good large but fails small.
  • Forgetting to check mobile.

Demo Ideas

  • Compare three auto-generated frames.
  • Choose the best frame and explain why.
  • Show a simple custom thumbnail.
  • Show thumbnail at small size.
  • Show title plus thumbnail together.

Area 13: Visibility And Publishing

Core Message

Visibility controls who can see the video. Beginners should understand public, private, unlisted, and scheduled before publishing.

What To Explain

Public:

  • Anyone can find and watch it.
  • It can appear on the channel, in search, and recommendations.

Private:

  • Only you and selected people can watch.
  • Useful for checking before release.

Unlisted:

  • Anyone with the link can watch.
  • It does not normally appear publicly on the channel or search.
  • Useful for sharing drafts.

Scheduled:

  • Publishes later at a chosen time.
  • Useful for consistency.

Beginner Mistakes

  • Leaving a video private and wondering why nobody sees it.
  • Sharing a private link with people who cannot access it.
  • Thinking unlisted means secure or secret.
  • Publishing before checking processing, title, and thumbnail.
  • Scheduling for the wrong time zone.

Demo Ideas

  • Upload a test video as private.
  • Change private to unlisted.
  • Explain when to use each setting.
  • Show a scheduled upload.

Good Video Titles

  • Public vs Private vs Unlisted on YouTube

  • How to Schedule a YouTube Video

Area 16: Basic YouTube Studio Analytics

Core Message

Beginners do not need to understand every metric. They need a small set of numbers that show whether the topic, title, thumbnail, and video held attention.

What To Explain

  • Impressions.
  • Click-through rate.
  • Views.
  • Average view duration.
  • Audience retention.
  • Subscribers gained.
  • Traffic sources.
  • Comments and viewer questions.

Simple Interpretation

Low impressions:

  • YouTube may not know who the video is for yet.
  • Topic may be too narrow or unclear.

Low click-through rate:

  • Title and thumbnail may not be strong enough.

Low retention:

  • Lead-in may be too slow.
  • Video may not deliver the promise quickly.

Good comments:

  • Viewers may be giving you future video ideas.

Beginner Mistakes

  • Judging a video after 30 minutes.
  • Obsessing over views only.
  • Changing title and thumbnail too often.
  • Ignoring comments with useful questions.
  • Copying another channel without understanding why it works.

Demo Ideas

  • Show a fake/simple analytics example.
  • Explain one video after 24 hours.
  • Explain one video after 7 days.
  • Turn comments into new video topics.

Good Video Titles

Area 18: Trust, Privacy, And Safety

Core Message

New YouTubers should learn early that not everything needs to be public.

What To Explain

  • Avoid showing private addresses.
  • Avoid showing full names, documents, account screens, banking screens, and exact locations.
  • Check screen recordings for private tabs, notifications, and file paths.
  • Be careful with family/private life.
  • Keep public claims accurate.
  • Correct mistakes openly when needed.

Beginner Mistakes

  • Screen recording personal information.
  • Showing location accidentally.
  • Leaving notifications visible.
  • Sharing too much too early.
  • Making claims without checking.

Demo Ideas

  • Show a screen recording privacy checklist.
  • Blur or crop private information.
  • Show phone Do Not Disturb before recording.
  • Explain why the Siberian land location will stay private.
