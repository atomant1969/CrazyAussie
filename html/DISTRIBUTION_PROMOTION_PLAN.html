Distribution And Promotion Plan

Parent: Video Roadmap

Related: Viral Video Strategy | Upload Checklist | Master Checklist

Purpose

Publishing to YouTube is only one step. Each useful video should be repackaged into smaller pieces for discovery, discussion, and traffic from other platforms.

The aim is to promote the channel without spamming. Each platform should receive a version that fits how people use that platform.

Core Workflow

For every main YouTube video:

  1. Publish the long-form YouTube video.
  2. Create 2-5 YouTube Shorts from the strongest moments.
  3. Write one short text post for X.
  4. Create one visual pin for Pinterest where the topic is searchable or evergreen.
  5. Create one TikTok clip where the topic has a strong short-form hook.
  6. Create one Facebook post or Reel where the topic fits.
  7. Save viewer questions and comments as future video ideas.
  8. Track which platform sends useful viewers back to YouTube.

Platform Roles

YouTube Shorts

Best for:

  • Testing hooks.
  • Reaching new viewers quickly.
  • Previewing long-form lessons.
  • Turning one tutorial into multiple entry points.

Use Shorts for:

  • One tip.
  • One mistake.
  • One before/after example.
  • One surprising monetization clarification.
  • One Siberian project bridge moment.

Call to action:

  • Soft CTA only.
  • Point to the full video when the Short is genuinely a cutdown or preview.

X.com

Best for:

  • Building in public.
  • Short lessons.
  • Public accountability.
  • Conversations with creators.
  • Posting milestones and process notes.

Post types:

  • One useful tip from the video.
  • A short thread breaking down the workflow.
  • A progress update on the 50k subscriber target.
  • A progress update on the $20,000 USD land fund.
  • A question for other creators.
  • A clip or screenshot with a direct link to the YouTube video.

Avoid:

  • Only dropping links.
  • Arguing about politics or sanctions.
  • Posting private location or banking details.

Pinterest

Best for:

  • Evergreen how-to content.
  • Checklists.
  • Beginner workflows.
  • Visual step-by-step pins.

Good pin topics:

  • Android phone YouTube setup checklist.
  • YouTube upload checklist.
  • Beginner editing workflow.
  • Thumbnail checklist.
  • YouTube monetization checklist.
  • Channel planning template.

Pinterest works better when the post has a useful visual. Make pins that look like helpful references, not random screenshots.

Facebook

Best for:

  • Groups, pages, Reels, and practical community discussion.
  • Beginner creator groups.
  • Homesteading or off-grid groups later, where allowed.
  • Sharing useful lessons with context.

Good Facebook approach:

  • Post a short useful summary first.
  • Link the video only where allowed and relevant.
  • Ask a real question.
  • Respect group rules.

Avoid:

  • Dropping links into groups without context.
  • Reposting the same text everywhere.
  • Turning every post into a sales pitch.

TikTok

Best for:

  • Fast hook testing.
  • Short tutorial clips.
  • Before/after edits.
  • Creator mistakes.
  • Public challenge updates.
  • Human moments from the Siberian project.

Good TikTok approach:

  • Open with the problem in the first second.
  • Keep the clip focused on one idea.
  • Use captions that are readable without sound.
  • Recut the clip for TikTok instead of only reposting a YouTube Short unchanged.
  • Point viewers to the full YouTube video only when the longer video genuinely expands the lesson.

Avoid:

  • Posting clips that attract the wrong audience.
  • Overusing trends that do not fit the channel.
  • Turning the channel into random entertainment if the main goal is trust, subscribers, and the land fund.

Other Useful Channels

Consider later:

  • Reddit, only where rules allow useful posts.
  • LinkedIn for creator/business process posts.
  • TikTok for short tutorial clips if the workflow can handle it.
  • Instagram Reels for short visual tips.
  • Email list later for people who want templates, updates, and land project progress.

Repurposing Template

From one 10-minute tutorial:

  • 1 YouTube long-form video.
  • 2-5 YouTube Shorts.
  • 1 X post.
  • 1 X thread if the topic is detailed.
  • 1 Pinterest checklist pin.
  • 1 TikTok clip.
  • 1 Facebook post or Reel.
  • 1 future FAQ idea from comments.
  • 1 update in the content tracker.

Weekly Promotion Rhythm

Minimum:

  • 1 long-form YouTube upload.
  • 2 Shorts.
  • 2 X posts.
  • 1 Pinterest pin.
  • 1 TikTok clip.
  • 1 Facebook post where appropriate.

Stretch:

  • 2 long-form videos.
  • 3-5 Shorts.
  • 4-7 X posts.
  • 2 Pinterest pins.
  • 2-3 TikTok clips.
  • 2 Facebook posts or Reels.

Promotion Checklist

Use this after each upload:

  • Create Shorts from the strongest moments.
  • Post one practical lesson on X.
  • Post one channel-building-in-public update when relevant.
  • Create a Pinterest checklist or visual summary when the topic is evergreen.
  • Create a TikTok clip from the strongest short-form moment.
  • Share to Facebook only where it is useful and allowed.
  • Save comments and replies as future video ideas.
  • Record traffic/referral results after 7 days.
  • Note whether the promotion brought subscribers, comments, or serious viewers.

Measurement

Track:

  • YouTube views from Shorts.
  • YouTube subscribers gained per video.
  • External traffic sources in YouTube Studio.
  • X replies, reposts, profile clicks, and link clicks where available.
  • Pinterest outbound clicks.
  • TikTok views, retention, profile clicks, comments, and followers.
  • Facebook comments, shares, and clicks where available.
  • Which posts create useful viewer questions.

Public Tone

Be useful first.

The best promotion for this channel is a steady trail of small, practical lessons:

  • "Here is the beginner mistake."
  • "Here is the simple fix."
  • "Here is the bigger project this supports."

That lets the channel promote itself without sounding desperate.
