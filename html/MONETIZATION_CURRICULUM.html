Purpose

This file explains the monetization education track for the channel. These videos should help new YouTubers understand how monetization works before they make expensive or irreversible decisions.

This is educational planning, not legal, tax, or financial advice. Before publishing any country-specific claim, recheck official Google/YouTube sources, local tax rules, and the relevant tax treaty.

Core Warning

Do not mix these up:

  1. YouTube revenue share: YouTube's platform split with creators.
  2. U.S. tax withholding: Tax Google may withhold on U.S.-sourced YouTube earnings for non-U.S. creators.
  3. MCN fee: A network's cut if the channel joins an MCN.
  4. Local tax: Tax the creator may owe in their own country of residence.
  5. Bank/payment fees: Currency conversion, transfer, and withdrawal costs.

The common "10% instead of 30%" discussion is usually about U.S. withholding tax treaty rates, not a YouTube commission.

Area 1: Monetization Table

Core Message

YouTube monetization is not one single switch. There are thresholds, features, review requirements, tax forms, payment setup, and policy rules.

Current YPP Structure To Explain

As of August 2026, YouTube has a two-stage Partner Program structure:

StageMain RequirementWatch RequirementFeatures
Early YPP / fan funding500 subscribers plus recent upload activity3,000 public watch hours in 365 days or 3 million Shorts views in 90 daysFan funding and some early monetization features where available
Full YPP / ad revenue1,000 subscribers4,000 public watch hours in 365 days or 10 million Shorts views in 90 daysWatch page ads, Shorts ads, YouTube Premium revenue, and broader monetization features

YouTube announced that from February 1, 2027, new full YPP applicants will need higher watch thresholds: 8,000 qualified watch hours in 365 days or 20 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days.

What To Explain In Videos

  • Subscribers alone are not enough.
  • Watch hours and Shorts views are different paths.
  • Shorts can grow subscribers but may not build long-form watch hours.
  • Meeting thresholds does not guarantee approval.
  • The channel must follow YouTube monetization policies.
  • Two-step verification and advanced features matter.
  • Monetization can be delayed by payment, tax, or identity setup.

Beginner Mistakes

  • Thinking 1,000 subscribers automatically means money.
  • Ignoring watch hours.
  • Building only Shorts traffic when the long-term channel needs long-form viewers.
  • Reusing content that fails originality checks.
  • Waiting until the last second to understand AdSense/tax setup.
  • Thinking ads shown on non-monetized videos mean the creator is being paid.

Area 2: What Counts Toward Monetization

Core Message

Not every view or hour helps the same way. New creators need to know which content supports the monetization path they are chasing.

What To Explain

  • Public long-form watch hours matter for the watch-hour path.
  • Shorts views matter for the Shorts path.
  • Private, deleted, or ineligible content may not help.
  • Livestreams, Shorts, and long-form have different strategic uses.
  • A channel that wants future documentary content should build long-form retention early.

Beginner Mistakes

  • Assuming every minute watched counts.
  • Deleting videos without understanding the effect.
  • Making only viral Shorts and then struggling with long-form.
  • Forgetting that public watch hours are time-limited to the recent 365-day window.
  • Forgetting Shorts views are time-limited to the recent 90-day window.

Area 3: Direct AdSense Route

Core Message

The direct route means the creator monetizes through YouTube Partner Program and receives payments through their own Google AdSense/AdSense for YouTube payment setup.

What To Explain

  • The channel applies through YouTube Studio.
  • The creator needs a valid payment profile.
  • Tax information must be submitted.
  • Identity/address/bank requirements must match the account setup.
  • The creator keeps control and avoids MCN fees.
  • Mistakes in country, tax, or identity setup can create serious problems.

Pros

  • No MCN percentage cut.
  • Direct relationship with YouTube/Google.
  • More control over payment profile.
  • Easier to leave because there is no network contract.
  • Cleaner long-term structure if the creator's country, ID, bank, and tax residence are straightforward.

Cons

  • The creator must handle setup correctly.
  • AdSense country, tax residency, identity, address, and bank details must be legitimate and consistent.
  • Some creators may not have usable documentation for the country they hoped to use.
  • Some countries or regions may have payment or program restrictions.
  • If the creator enters false information, the account can be rejected, delayed, or worse.

Beginner Mistakes

  • Choosing a country because the rate looks better, not because they are legally resident there.
  • Using a friend's address without understanding tax and identity implications.
  • Assuming any bank account in a country is enough.
  • Uploading ID from one country while claiming tax/payment setup in another.
  • Waiting until monetization approval to learn about tax forms.

Area 4: MCN Route

Core Message

An MCN can be useful when a creator needs help with payout routing, CMS linking, Content ID, or regional complications, but it is a business contract, not free money.

What To Explain

  • MCN means Multi-Channel Network.
  • Some MCNs link channels through a managed CMS.
  • The MCN may receive revenue and pay the creator after taking a fee.
  • Fees can vary.
  • Contract terms matter.
  • Exit rules matter.
  • The MCN may or may not help enough to justify its cut.

Pros

  • May solve payout routing problems.
  • May support creators who cannot easily use direct AdSense.
  • May provide support, music, Content ID, or rights tools.
  • May be practical for creators filming from complicated regions.
  • May let the creator use real ID while the network handles corporate payment rails.

Cons

  • Network fee reduces revenue.
  • Contract lock-in can be painful.
  • Some networks provide weak support.
  • Exiting can be slow or difficult.
  • The creator may lose flexibility.
  • A bad MCN can be worse than no MCN.

Questions To Teach Viewers To Ask

  • What percentage do you take?
  • Is the percentage taken before or after YouTube's share?
  • What payment methods do you support?
  • What countries and banks can you pay?
  • What documents do you require?
  • Is personal AdSense required?
  • Do you use managed CMS?
  • What is the contract term?
  • What is the exit process?
  • Are sponsorships, affiliates, donations, and merch outside the MCN fee?
  • Who controls Content ID?
  • What support do you actually provide?

Beginner Mistakes

  • Signing the first MCN that replies.
  • Thinking MCN approval replaces YouTube policy compliance.
  • Ignoring the exit clause.
  • Not calculating the fee over a full year.
  • Giving up a percentage for services they do not need.

Area 5: U.S. Tax Withholding For Non-U.S. Creators

Core Message

For creators outside the United States, Google may withhold U.S. tax from U.S.-sourced YouTube earnings. Submitting tax information can affect the rate. A tax treaty may reduce the withholding rate if the creator is eligible.

What To Explain

  • YouTube earnings from U.S. viewers can be treated as U.S.-sourced income.
  • Non-U.S. creators generally submit tax info in AdSense.
  • The form is commonly W-8BEN for individuals or W-8BEN-E for entities.
  • Without valid tax info, withholding can be much worse.
  • With tax info but no treaty benefit, the rate may still be high.
  • With a valid treaty claim, the rate may be reduced depending on country, income type, and eligibility.
  • This is separate from YouTube's platform revenue share and separate from MCN fees.

The 30% vs 10% Example

Use this carefully:

  • 30% is commonly discussed as a default U.S. withholding rate on U.S.-sourced earnings when no treaty benefit applies.
  • 10% may apply for some treaty situations, including some royalty categories for some countries, if the creator is genuinely eligible.
  • This is not a universal rule and not a "YouTube commission."
  • The rate depends on tax residency, treaty, form, income type, and the information accepted in AdSense.

Kazakhstan Example

Kazakhstan may be attractive to discuss because some creators look at treaty withholding outcomes and international banking access.

What to say:

  • Kazakhstan should only be used if the creator has a legitimate legal/tax basis to use it.
  • A Kazakhstan bank account alone does not automatically make someone eligible for Kazakhstan treaty benefits.
  • The creator must verify tax residency, ID, payment profile, and treaty eligibility.
  • Treaty-rate claims should be checked in AdSense and against official tax treaty sources before relying on them.

Other Potentially Advantageous Jurisdictions

Do not publish a "best countries" list as advice. Instead, teach viewers how to evaluate a country:

  • Is YouTube Partner Program available there?
  • Can the creator legally reside or register there?
  • Can the creator prove tax residency?
  • Is there a U.S. tax treaty?
  • What treaty rate may apply to YouTube/royalty income?
  • Can the creator open and maintain a bank account?
  • Are Google payments supported?
  • What local taxes apply?
  • Are there currency controls or transfer problems?
  • Does the setup create legal risk or false-information risk?

Countries that often deserve research if the creator has a legitimate connection:

  • Australia.
  • United Kingdom.
  • Canada.
  • Ireland.
  • New Zealand.
  • Kazakhstan.
  • Georgia.
  • Armenia.
  • Kyrgyzstan.
  • United Arab Emirates.
  • Estonia or other EU countries.

These are not recommendations. They are research candidates because creators often compare treaty access, banking access, business setup, residency rules, and payment practicality.

Beginner Mistakes

  • Calling withholding a YouTube fee.
  • Looking only at one percentage and ignoring local tax.
  • Assuming a bank account equals tax residency.
  • Assuming a friend's address is enough.
  • Choosing a country they cannot document.
  • Ignoring exchange rates and bank fees.
  • Forgetting that MCN payouts may have a different structure.

Area 7: Alternative Monetization Before YPP

Core Message

Creators do not have to wait for ad revenue to think like a business, but early monetization must not damage trust.

Options

Affiliate links:

  • Good for tools/software genuinely used.
  • Requires clear disclosure.
  • Should not turn reviews into fake recommendations.

Sponsorships:

  • Harder early.
  • Can work if the channel has a specific audience.
  • Must be disclosed.

Digital products:

  • Checklists, templates, guides.
  • Good fit for beginner YouTube education.

Consulting/coaching:

  • Possible later.
  • Time-intensive.
  • Needs credibility.

Fan support:

  • May work once the Siberian project has emotional pull.
  • Should not be the first assumption.

Beginner Mistakes

  • Taking bad sponsorships too early.
  • Promoting tools they do not use.
  • Hiding affiliate relationships.
  • Making every video feel like an ad.
  • Selling before building trust.
