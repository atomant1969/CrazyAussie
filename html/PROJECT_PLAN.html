Project Goal

Build a serious YouTube channel that first attracts beginner creators through editing software reviews, editing tutorials, and practical YouTube production lessons, then gradually transitions into the main documentary project: an Australian building a homestead in Siberia.

The first business objective is to earn $20,000 USD total creator revenue so the land can be bought for the off-grid Siberian project. Subscriber growth is the engine, but the land fund is the mission-critical target.

The strategic subscriber target before the full transition is 50,000 subscribers, ideally within 3 months. This is aggressive, so the launch must be focused on searchable, useful, repeatable lead-generation content that can also support revenue through YPP, MCN payouts, affiliates, sponsorships, digital products, or other trust-safe income.

Growth should not rely on uploads alone. Each major video needs a viral packaging check and a distribution plan across YouTube Shorts, TikTok, X.com, Pinterest, Facebook, and any other platform that can bring useful viewers back to the channel.

Core Channel Identity

  • Channel name: Not locked yet. Working name: The Crazy Aussie in Siberia.
  • Long-term topic: Documentary-style Siberian land, building, banya, cabin, and homestead project.
  • Starting topic: Editing software reviews and tutorials for new YouTubers.
  • Audience focus: Builders, homesteaders, practical creators, and other beginners who want to learn YouTube.
  • Tone: Serious, practical, direct, credible, with dry Aussie humor where it fits.
  • Avoid: Joke-first branding, exaggerated survival claims, cheap clickbait, and random trend chasing.
  • Privacy: Keep exact land location, family/private life, politics, sanctions, and banking details out of public content.

Positioning Statement

The Crazy Aussie in Siberia helps practical beginners learn how to edit and produce YouTube videos while documenting the preparation for a real Siberian homestead build.

This lets the channel earn attention before the land project is fully ready, while still building a natural bridge into the long-term documentary.

Audience Strategy

Phase 1 Audience: Beginner YouTubers

These viewers want to start a channel but feel blocked by production basics.

They need help with:

  • Which editing software to use.
  • How to make a first edit.
  • How to cut mistakes cleanly.
  • How to export correctly.
  • How to make Shorts from longer videos.
  • How to organize footage.
  • How to structure a video before editing.
  • How to upload with confidence.

Phase 2 Audience: Serious Project Followers

These viewers stay for the larger Siberian story.

They care about:

  • Off-grid building.
  • Tools and materials.
  • Rural Siberian life without exposing private location details.
  • Climate adaptation.
  • Banya, cabin, and homestead construction.
  • A foreigner learning local systems.
  • Long-term project progress.

Channel Phases

Phase 0: Foundation

Purpose: Set up the channel, repo, planning system, templates, and production workflow.

Success criteria:

  • Channel identity is clear.
  • First 20 video ideas are planned.
  • Repeatable script, shoot, edit, upload, and review workflow exists.
  • First videos can be recorded with an Android phone and edited on a computer.
  • Viral packaging and off-platform promotion checklists exist.

Phase 1: Lead Generation Through Editing Tutorials

Purpose: Grow the first audience by solving real problems for new YouTubers and build the revenue base for the $20,000 USD land fund.

Core video types:

  • Editing software reviews for beginners.
  • Editing app comparisons.
  • First-edit walkthroughs.
  • Export settings.
  • Cutting mistakes.
  • Audio cleanup inside the editor.
  • Captions and basic text.
  • Shorts editing.
  • Simple intros and outros.
  • Preparing a video for upload.
  • Viral beginner mistakes and simple fixes.
  • Shorts cutdowns designed to funnel back to long-form tutorials.

Important rule: These videos can be practical and search-focused first. The Siberian project should appear as a light identity bridge, not as a forced topic in every tutorial before footage exists.

Phase 2: Bridge Content

Purpose: Slowly train the audience to expect the Siberian build story.

Bridge video types:

  • Why this channel starts with editing tutorials.
  • Building a YouTube channel before the Siberian build begins.
  • My 3-month plan to reach 50k subscribers.
  • How I am preparing to document a homestead project.
  • How to edit outdoor/build footage once the project starts.
  • How to film a documentary alone.
  • What a 50-video content plan looks like before a major project starts.

Goal: Keep helping new YouTubers, but make the Siberian project impossible to miss.

Phase 3: 50k Subscriber Transition

Purpose: Once the channel has meaningful audience momentum and a credible path toward the $20,000 USD land fund, start moving toward the land/build focus.

Transition principles:

  • Do not abruptly abandon the production audience.
  • Keep production lessons, but make the Siberian project the main example.
  • Introduce build episodes as documentary case studies.
  • Explain the shift honestly to the audience, including that the tutorial phase was designed to fund the land purchase.
  • Use polls and comments to identify who is interested in the build arc.

Suggested transition mix:

  • 50% editing tutorials/reviews.
  • 30% Siberian project preparation.
  • 20% personal/channel updates.

Then slowly move toward:

  • 30% YouTube editing/production lessons.
  • 60% Siberian build documentary.
  • 10% monetization/channel operations.

Phase 4: Land and Build Documentary

Purpose: Begin the full documentary project once the land funding target has been met or another safe funding path is confirmed.

Build order:

  1. Land access, clearing, fence/gate, or first work area.
  2. First structure decision.
  3. Tool storage or shed if that becomes the priority.
  4. Banya if practical needs make it first.
  5. Cabin or temporary living base.
  6. Main house.

Documentary style:

  • Clear opening explanation.
  • Calm technical breakdown.
  • Work sequence with strong natural sound.
  • Honest problem solving.
  • Final lesson and next step.

Phase 5: Monetization

Purpose: Apply for monetization once the channel has traction and the payout route is clear, then turn that monetization into the $20,000 USD land fund.

Current YouTube structure, verified August 17, 2026:

  • Early YPP/fan funding tier: 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in the last 90 days, and either 3,000 qualified public watch hours in the last 12 months or 3 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days.
  • Full YPP/ad revenue tier: 1,000 subscribers and either 4,000 qualified public watch hours in the last 12 months or 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days.
  • YouTube has announced that from February 1, 2027, new creators applying for full YPP will need 8,000 qualified watch hours in the last 365 days or 20 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days.

Because the channel aims for 50,000 subscribers before the main monetization push, the practical focus should be:

  • Build a large English-speaking audience.
  • Keep content original and policy-safe.
  • Track watch hours from long-form videos.
  • Track total creator revenue and the remaining gap to $20,000 USD.
  • Treat an MCN as the likely monetization route.
  • Research MCNs before signing anything.
  • Avoid relying on Russian ad inventory.
  • Confirm the payout path before applying or linking accounts.

Monetization Route Options

Direct YPP Route

Pros:

  • Keeps all revenue after YouTube's platform share.
  • No MCN fee.
  • Direct relationship with YouTube.

Risks:

  • Identity, location, bank, address, and AdSense requirements must align.
  • Regional restrictions may complicate setup.
  • Mistakes in account setup can create delays or rejection.

MCN Route

Pros:

  • May solve payout routing and account-country issues.
  • Can provide managed CMS support.
  • May offer payment methods that direct AdSense cannot.

Risks:

  • Network fee.
  • Contract lock-in.
  • Weak support from some networks.
  • Exit problems if the agreement is poor.
  • Reduced control over some monetization operations.

MCN Due Diligence Questions

Ask before signing:

  • What percentage do you take?
  • Is the fee on gross revenue or net received revenue?
  • What payout methods are available?
  • Can you pay to my Kazakhstan bank account?
  • What documents do you require from me as an Australian citizen?
  • What is the contract term?
  • What is the notice period to leave?
  • Are there penalties for leaving?
  • Do you require exclusivity?
  • Do you control Content ID claims?
  • Do you place any restrictions on sponsors?
  • How quickly do you pay after YouTube pays you?
  • What happens if YouTube changes YPP requirements again?

Risks

Audience Mismatch

Risk: Editing tutorial viewers may not all care about Siberian building.

Mitigation: Keep the working identity visible from the beginning, then increase Siberian bridge content once the audience has formed.

Algorithm Confusion

Risk: YouTube may struggle to classify the channel if editing tutorials and homestead documentaries are mixed too abruptly.

Mitigation: Sequence the transition slowly: editing software, broader production, channel building in public, Siberian preparation, then build documentary.

Monetization Restrictions

Risk: AdSense, country, payment, sanctions, and MCN rules may change.

Mitigation: Discuss monetization publicly at a high level, but keep politics, sanctions mechanics, banking details, private life, and exact location out of videos.

Production Burnout

Risk: Two long videos per week may be too much.

Mitigation: Batch record, use repeatable templates, and turn one recording session into long-form plus Shorts.

Operating Principle

Every video should do at least one of these jobs:

  1. Help a new YouTuber edit or publish a better video.
  2. Build trust in the Crazy Aussie in Siberia project.
  3. Move the audience closer to caring about the land/build journey.
  4. Prepare the channel for monetization without rushing into a bad payout setup.
  5. Move the project closer to the $20,000 USD land purchase target.
  6. Create a reusable clip, post, pin, or discussion point for promotion.
